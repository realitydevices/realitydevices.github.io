# Inbound as a Hand-Placed Item

## Concept

There is no contact form. One item on the sphere — placed deliberately, like
any other — carries the maintainer's email address and a single line stating
what is worth his attention. No "Get in touch", no CTA, no form fields.
Finding it requires the same exploration as finding a post. The framing is
that Kirk reads carefully and selectively, not that he is optimising for
inbound volume.

## Principles advanced / tensioned

- Advances **PURPOSE #4** directly: the site is shaped to *receive* an
  opportunity, not *convert* one. A form would push it toward sales-site
  shape; a hand-placed item does not.
- Advances **§4 (curated, deterministic, hand-placed)**: this is the
  strongest possible expression of the principle — even the contact surface
  is curated.
- Anti-LinkedIn by construction. No "Open to opportunities" badge, no DM
  button, no inbound-funnel pattern.

## Open before any code

- Exact wording of the one-line filter (this *is* the spec — voice carries
  the whole item).
- Whether the email is `mailto:` or rendered text only (rendered text
  reinforces the "be deliberate about reaching out" framing; `mailto:` is
  more conventional).
- Where on the sphere it sits relative to writing and links — close enough
  to be findable, far enough that it isn't the first thing seen.
